iPhone Hidden Album vs. a private photo vault

Use Apple’s Hidden album for convenient, built-in privacy. Use a separate photo vault when you need a different PIN, app disguises, decoy access or protection from someone who knows your device passcode.

Short answer

The Hidden album and a private photo vault solve different problems. Hidden is excellent for removing media from normal Photos views. A vault adds a separate security boundary when the person holding the phone can already unlock it.

Apple has made the Hidden album substantially more private than it used to be. On current iOS versions it is locked by default, requires Face ID, Touch ID or the device passcode, and can be removed from view inside Photos. For many people, that is enough.

The key limitation is credential separation: Apple’s Hidden album belongs to the same device security system. If another person knows the iPhone passcode, they may also be able to unlock Hidden. A dedicated vault can require its own PIN and add controls Photos does not provide.

When Apple’s Hidden album is the right choice

Choose Hidden when you want zero setup, trust your device passcode, and mainly need to prevent private images from appearing in the Library, albums or Photos widget. It is built into iOS, works across devices with iCloud Photos, and does not require another app.

To hide an item, press and hold it in Photos, choose Hide and confirm. You can also turn off Show Hidden Album in Settings → Apps → Photos so the collection is not visible in Photos. Apple notes that hidden items sync as hidden across devices when iCloud Photos is enabled.

Hidden does not mean deleted. The photo remains part of your Apple Photos library and iCloud Photos environment. This is useful for continuity, but it is different from moving an encrypted copy into a separate app.

When a private photo vault is the better fit

Choose a vault when the risk begins after the phone is already unlocked. Common examples include a partner who knows the passcode, a child borrowing the phone, a repair handoff, or a coworker using the device for a task.

A well-designed vault should explain where files are stored, how they are encrypted, what happens to originals, whether biometrics fall back to the device passcode, and how recovery works. Do not choose solely by a “military-grade” marketing phrase.

VaultBox uses AES-256-GCM encryption, a separate PIN, biometric-only Face ID authentication, utility covers, a decoy PIN, break-in alerts and optional encrypted backup in the user’s iCloud account. It does not hide or lock other installed apps.

Choose based on your actual threat model

Choose Hidden if:

  • No one else knows your iPhone passcode.
  • You want the simplest built-in option.
  • You are comfortable keeping the media in Apple Photos and iCloud Photos.
  • You do not need a decoy library, separate PIN or documents.

Choose a private vault if:

  • Someone else knows or regularly sees your device passcode.
  • You want a PIN that is different from the phone’s passcode.
  • You need a calculator cover, decoy access or break-in records.
  • You need to protect documents as well as photos and videos.

Use both if:

You can keep low-sensitivity images in Hidden for convenience and reserve a separate vault for the smaller set that needs credential separation. Avoid creating unnecessary duplicates, and keep track of which copy is authoritative.

If another person knowing your passcode creates a safety risk, address the passcode first. A vault can add separation, but it cannot secure the rest of your device or Apple Account. Apple’s Safety Check can review sharing, connected devices and account access.
